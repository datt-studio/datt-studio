The name came first. The mark took considerably longer.

## The name

We are two people, one Vietnamese and one who spent three and a half years in Vietnam. We wanted something that would sit properly in both places. A name that was ours rather than borrowed from either.

DATT came from two directions at once. In Vietnamese, Đạt means to reach or to attain. In English it sounds close to that, which is a word people already trust to mean a standard rather than a boast. That studio. The kind of mark you would want to be held to.

Both readings point the same way. We moved from Vietnam to the United Kingdom, and the name carries the movement and the intent. Reaching, attaining, through the work and the craft.

## The first mark

The first mark had a D with two squares on the stem. The two squares were the studio and the work. The negative square in the middle was the client, sitting in the space where the two met. It was a legible idea, and the geometry held together.

We registered it.

## Living with it

Then we kept looking at it.

Nothing was wrong with it in the way a broken thing is wrong. The letterforms worked. The idea explained itself. It simply did not feel like enough to put in front of people we wanted to work with. It did not feel like something we would be proud to sign.

The trigger was not a client, or a competitor, or seeing it somewhere it did not belong. It was time. We had both registered it and neither of us wanted to use it. When a mark is comfortable to keep and not comfortable to show, that is an answer, and it arrives slowly.

## The mark we have now

The current mark is a D and an A with two T's joined behind them.

The A carries a forward sweep, and the bowl of the D carries the same sweep. The corners stay cut rather than rounded, so the mark holds an edge under movement instead of softening it. That is the motion of the mark: forward momentum, always reaching, and a form that stays precise while it moves. The two T's connect and hold. The mark is doing something, and the thing it is doing is holding itself together while it moves.

It clicked. Both how it looked and what it meant, arriving at the same time, which does not happen often enough to take for granted.

## What we would tell ourselves at the start

Register the first mark. Not because it is the mark, but because a trademark stops anyone else taking the thing you have already built, and a thing you have already built is worth holding before someone else decides what it looks like. That first registration is still live, and it held while the mark moved on, which was the point of registering something we were not finished with.

Do not confuse having a decision with having the decision. We had a registered mark, a live site and a business name, and the honest position was that the identity was unfinished.

Live with the work longer than is comfortable. That is where the judgement came from, and nowhere else. Nobody told us the first mark was weak. We did.

Know the standard you are stopping at. We could have kept redrawing indefinitely, and at some point that stops being rigour and becomes restlessness. The mark we have is not finished because it is perfect. It is finished because it meets a standard we could name, and because we both recognised the point where further changes were taste rather than judgement.

## Where this is written down

None of this was tested with a poll or a survey. It was a judgement two people had to agree on, and they did. The Vietnamese reading of the name was checked with someone who grew up speaking it, and the rest of it is us, describing a decision we were not sure about and are now glad we made.

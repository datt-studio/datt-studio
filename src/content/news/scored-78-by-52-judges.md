The FWA runs a live judging window. Ours was open for 14 days. Fifty-two jury members scored the site across the window, and the score closed at 78.

We did not win Site of the Day. The FWA publishes winners, that is how their showcase works, and we are completely at peace with it.

Because winners are the only ones listed, most people will never see the score. Which is the reason for this article.

## What the score says

Fifty-two judges looked at this site carefully, on their own time, and independently arrived at the same number. That is a measure of the work itself, taken by jury members who do this every day and have no reason to be generous.

The line is not flat. It opened at 79, held there for the first few days, and settled at 78 as the window closed. The FWA recalculates the score as votes arrive, so the figure moves while judging runs, and where it closes is what the result records.

78 out of 100 is a strong result. It is also not a top score, and every entry was judged against the same standard.

Judging is subjective by nature, and a panel may favour a different kind of site. Some reward heavy interactivity, some reward a particular technology or stack, some reward a different visual expression. Our work is deliberately restrained, deliberately fast, deliberately quiet, and that is a choice with a cost. Some juries reward that. Some do not. Site of the Day is one jury's call on one day.

What 52 scores tell us is not that the site is any less. It is that the work holds up under a serious panel, and that is worth saying out loud.

## Thank you to the jury

Fifty-two jury members gave their time to score an entry that was never going to be published on their site. That is the part we find remarkable. The jury did work with no coverage, no link, no badge, because the work was the point.

So thank you. To every member of The FWA jury who opened the site, spent time with it, and scored it, thank you for the time and the consideration. A score of 78 from 52 judges is a serious measure of the work, and we do not take it lightly.

## The same work, a second jury

We submitted the same site to Awwwards, and it is officially a nominee. The nomination is dated 9 October 2026. We are waiting on the jury decision, so we do not yet know whether it goes as far as an honourable mention or Site of the Day.

Our community votes are in. Thank you to everyone who took the time to support DATT STUDIO and cast a vote. Every one of you gave us something we could not have built ourselves, and we are grateful for all of it.

## What happens next

We are proud of what we have built, and proud of the outcome. Not because a number is high, but because the work is good, and because it was built properly, by us, with restraint and intent.

To everyone who has supported us on this journey, thank you. To the community, the jury, and everyone who visited, read, shared, or wrote to us, we are grateful.

We remain hopeful about what comes next. And whatever the results say, the direction does not change. We will keep building great work, doing it properly, and getting better at it as we grow.

Nothing unnecessary remains.
